When the exponent is not a multiple

Finding the cycle is the easy half. The half that catches people is working out where a big exponent lands inside it — especially when it does not land neatly at the end.

The cycle itself

Units digits of the powers of 3:

3¹ = 3      3⁵ = 243  → 3
3² = 9      3⁶ = 729  → 9
3³ = 27 → 7 3⁷        → 7
3⁴ = 81 → 1 3⁸        → 1

cycle:  3, 9, 7, 1   then it repeats.   LENGTH 4

So the units digit of 3ⁿ depends only on where n sits in a repeating block of four.

The step that catches people

Line the exponents up against their position in the block:

n:        1  2  3  4  5  6  7  8
digit:    3  9  7  1  3  9  7  1
position: 1  2  3  4  1  2  3  4

The position is the remainder when n is divided by 4 — with one trap. When n is an exact multiple of 4 the remainder is 0, and there is no position 0; that case is the last entry, position 4.

The rule Divide the exponent by the cycle length. A remainder of 1, 2 or 3 sends you to that position. A remainder of 0 sends you to the end of the cycle, not the start.

Worked, off the multiple

Units digit of 3¹¹ ?

cycle 3, 9, 7, 1 — length 4
11 ÷ 4 = 2 remainder 3
remainder 3 → third entry → 7

And one with a different base, to show the method is the same:

Units digit of 8¹⁷ ?

8¹ = 8   8² = 64 → 4   8³ = 512 → 2   8⁴ → 6
cycle 8, 4, 2, 6 — length 4
17 ÷ 4 = 4 remainder 1
remainder 1 → first entry → 8

Notice you never computed 8¹⁷. You computed four small powers and did one division. That is the entire technique, and it does not get harder as the exponent grows.

The same thing with remainders instead of digits

Units digits are just remainders after dividing by 10. Nothing changes for other divisors:

Remainder of 2ⁿ when divided by 7?

2¹ = 2   2² = 4   2³ = 8 → 1   2⁴ = 16 → 2
cycle 2, 4, 1 — LENGTH 3, not 4

So divide the exponent by 3 this time.

The cycle length is whatever it turns out to be. Find it, then divide by it.

Practice

  1. Units digit of 3⁹?
    Answer

    9 ÷ 4 = 2 remainder 1 → first entry → 3.

  2. Units digit of 7¹⁰?
    Answer

    Cycle 7, 9, 3, 1. 10 ÷ 4 = 2 remainder 2 → 9.

  3. Units digit of 4²¹?
    Answer

    Cycle 4, 6 — length 2. 21 ÷ 2 = 10 remainder 1 → 4.

  4. Remainder of 2¹² divided by 7?
    Answer

    Cycle 2, 4, 1 — length 3. 12 ÷ 3 = 4 remainder 0, so the end of the cycle → 1.
