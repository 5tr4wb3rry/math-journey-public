Degree, and the handshake lemma

Every edge has two ends. That single fact halves every count in this topic — and running it backwards is how you find a number nobody gave you.

What degree is

The degree of a vertex is the number of edges touching it. In a friendship graph, it is how many friends that person has. In a handshake graph, it is how many hands that person shook. 

    ●               ●
    │               ╱ ╲
    │         degree 2   degree 3

  degree 1   degree 3   degree 1

The handshake lemma

Six people are in a room. The number of people each one shook hands with is 2, 2, 3, 3, 1, and 1. How many handshakes happened?

Add the degrees: 2 + 2 + 3 + 3 + 1 + 1 = 12. But every handshake was counted twice — once for each person in it. So the actual number of handshakes is: 

sum of degrees ÷ 2 = edges

12 ÷ 2 = 6 handshakes
Why it works Each edge contributes exactly 1 to the degree of each of its two endpoints. So the sum of all degrees counts every edge twice — once at each end. That is all the lemma says.

A fact hiding in plain sight

The sum of all degrees is always even, because it is 2 times the number of edges. So: 

sum of degrees = 2 × (number of edges)

If you ever add up everyone's handshakes and get an odd number, something is wrong — you have miscounted, or the problem is impossible.

Parity check The sum of degrees is always even. This is a free check on any problem in this topic: add the degrees, and if the sum is odd, the situation cannot exist.

Running it backwards

In a group, every single person shook hands with exactly 3 other people, and there were 9 handshakes altogether. How many people were in the group?

This is the hard problem from the assessment — the lemma pointed the other way. Nobody tells you the sum of degrees; they tell you the answer (9 handshakes) and the degree each person has (3). You have to find the number of people.

Let n be the number of people. Each shakes 3 hands, so the sum of degrees is 3n. The handshake lemma says: 

sum of degrees = 2 × edges
      3n         = 2 × 9
      3n         = 18
      n          = 6

Six people. Each shakes 3 hands, total degree sum = 18, edges = 18 ÷ 2 = 9. It checks.

Easy to miss 3n must be even for the problem to have a whole-number answer — because 3n equals 2 × edges. So n must be even. If someone says "each person shook 3 hands, total 10 handshakes", that is impossible: 3n = 20 gives n = 20/3, not a whole number.

Another one backwards

At a party, everyone shook hands with exactly 4 other people, and there were 20 handshakes in total. How many people were at the party? 

4n = 2 × 20
4n = 40
 n = 10

Ten people. Degree sum = 40, edges = 20. Checks.

Practice

  1. Five people. Degrees are 3, 2, 2, 1, 2. How many edges?
    Answer

    Sum = 3 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 2 = 10. Edges = 10 ÷ 2 = 5.

  2. Everyone in a group of 7 people shook exactly 2 hands. How many handshakes happened?
    Answer

    Degree sum = 7 × 2 = 14. Edges = 14 ÷ 2 = 7.

  3. Everyone shook exactly 5 hands, and there were 20 handshakes. How many people?
    Answer

    5n = 2 × 20 = 40, so n = 8.

  4. Everyone shook exactly 4 hands, and there were 14 handshakes. How many people?
    Answer

    4n = 2 × 14 = 28, so n = 7.

  5. Can a group of 5 people each shake hands with exactly 3 others? ( Answer yes or no, and say why. )
    Answer

    5 × 3 = 15, which is odd. The sum of degrees must be even, so no — this is impossible.
