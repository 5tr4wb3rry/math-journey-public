Lesson · Rate, work, and mixture problems

Rates add. Times don't.

One sentence carries almost this entire topic. Once you believe it, the problems stop being about pipes and painters and become bookkeeping.

Start here

One pipe fills a pool in 4 hours.
Another fills the same pool in 6 hours.
Running together, how long do they take?

The tempting answers are 10 hours and 5 hours. Both are wrong, and it is worth being precise about why, because the reason points straight at the method.

10 hours is adding the times. But two pipes running together must be faster than either alone — you cannot add help and get a slower result. Any answer above 4 is impossible before you calculate anything.

5 hours is the average. Also impossible, for the same reason: it is slower than the 4-hour pipe working by itself.

Free sanity check Working together must beat the fastest worker alone. The answer here has to be under 4 hours. That single observation kills most wrong answers before you commit to them.

Why times refuse to add

Times are not the thing that combines. If you work for 4 hours and I work for 6, the job did not take 10 hours — we were working at the same time.

What genuinely combines is how much gets done per hour. That is the rate, and rates do add: in one hour, the pool receives what the first pipe delivers plus what the second delivers. 

pipe A: fills the pool in 4 hours  →  rate = 1/4 of a pool per hour
pipe B: fills the pool in 6 hours  →  rate = 1/6 of a pool per hour

together: 1/4 + 1/6 = 3/12 + 2/12 = 5/12 of a pool per hour

A time in hours becomes a rate in pools-per-hour by flipping it. That flip is the only real move in the topic — and it has to happen twice.

Flipping back

5/12 is a genuine result, but read what it is: 5/12 of a pool per hour. The question did not ask for a fraction of a pool. It asked for hours. 

rate     = 5/12 pools per hour
time     = 1 ÷ (5/12)  =  12/5 hours  =  2.4 hours
Easy to miss Stopping at 5/12. The arithmetic is finished but the question is not answered — 5/12 is a speed, and the question wants a duration. Flipping in gets you the rate; you have to flip back out to get the time.

Check it against the sanity test: 2.4 hours is under 4, so it survives. And the units confirm it — pools ÷ (pools per hour) leaves hours. 

The shape of every problem in this family:

  times  →  flip  →  rates  →  add  →  flip  →  time
                                        ↑
                        this last flip is the one that gets dropped

A second one, all the way through

A tap fills a tank in 3 hours; a drain empties it in 6 hours.
Both open, how long to fill?

fill rate  = 1/3 tank per hour
drain rate = 1/6 tank per hour, but it works AGAINST you → subtract

net = 1/3 − 1/6 = 2/6 − 1/6 = 1/6 tank per hour
time = 1 ÷ (1/6) = 6 hours

Anything working against the job enters with a minus sign. Everything else is identical.

Working backwards

A alone takes 6 hours. A and B together take 2 hours. How long does B alone take?

together rate = 1/2   (this is a rate, from flipping the 2 hours)
A's rate      = 1/6
B's rate      = 1/2 − 1/6 = 3/6 − 1/6 = 2/6 = 1/3

B alone: 1 ÷ (1/3) = 3 hours

Same three flips. Notice that "2 hours" had to become a rate before it was usable — that is the first flip doing its job in the opposite direction.

Your turn

  1. Two pipes fill a tank in 2 hours and 3 hours. Together?
    Answer6/5 hours, or 1.2 hours. Rates: 1/2 + 1/3 = 5/6 per hour; flip back to get 6/5. Under 2, so it passes the sanity check.
  2. Two machines take 5 hours and 20 hours. Together?
    Answer4 hours. 1/5 + 1/20 = 4/20 + 1/20 = 5/20 = 1/4 per hour; flip to get 4.
  3. A tap fills a bath in 10 minutes; the plug leaks it empty in 15 minutes. Tap on, plug out — how long to fill?
    Answer30 minutes. 1/10 − 1/15 = 3/30 − 2/30 = 1/30 per minute; flip to get 30.
  4. Three identical pumps each drain a pond in 9 hours. All three together?
    Answer3 hours. Three rates of 1/9 give 3/9 = 1/3 per hour; flip to get 3.
  5. Harder: A and B together take 4 hours. A alone takes 12 hours. A works alone for 6 hours, then B joins. How much longer to finish?
    Answer2 hours more. B's rate is 1/4 − 1/12 = 1/6. In 6 hours alone, A does 6 × 1/12 = 1/2 the job, leaving 1/2. Together they do 1/4 per hour, so the remaining half takes (1/2) ÷ (1/4) = 2 hours.

What the check will ask

Combined-work problems where the answer is a time, including one with something working against the job and one running backwards from a combined time. Different numbers throughout. Before submitting any of them, say out loud whether the answer is plausible.
